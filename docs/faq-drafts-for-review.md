## 1. 7-days-bali-first-timers

**Is 7 days enough for a first trip to Bali?** Yes. A week lets first-timers see Bali's four main sides — Ubud's culture and rice terraces, Canggu's beaches and cafés, a Nusa Penida day trip, and Uluwatu's clifftop temples — without rushing, as long as you base yourself in two or three areas rather than moving hotels nightly.
**What's the best area to stay in Bali for first-timers?** Split your stay: Ubud for culture and nature, then Canggu or Seminyak for beach and nightlife, finishing near Uluwatu for clifftop sunsets. Two or three bases beat one hub, because Bali's traffic makes long daily drives slow and tiring.
★ **How do I get from Bali to Nusa Penida?** Fast boats run from Sanur to Nusa Penida in about 30–45 minutes, departing several times daily from roughly IDR 250,000–400,000 (about US$16–26) return. Start early — afternoon seas get choppy. A day tour with hotel pickup is the easiest option for first-timers.
**Do I need a visa, and is there a tourist tax for Bali?** Most nationalities get a Visa on Arrival: the e-VOA costs IDR 500,000 (about US$35) for 30 days, extendable once. Bali also charges a one-time tourist levy of IDR 150,000 (about US$10). Pay both online before you fly to skip airport queues.
**When is the best time to visit Bali?** April to October (the dry season) is best, with sunny days and calm seas ideal for Nusa Penida and Uluwatu. July–August is busiest and priciest; May, June and September give the best balance of good weather and thinner crowds.
**How much does a week in Bali cost?** Budget travellers manage from about US$40–50/day (guesthouses, local food, scooters); mid-range runs US$90–130/day with nicer stays and private drivers. This excludes international flights but includes the Nusa Penida day trip and the Bali tourist levy.

## 2. 5-days-bali-ubud-canggu-uluwatu

**Is 5 days enough for Bali?** Yes, for a focused first taste. Five days comfortably covers Ubud (rice terraces, temples, waterfalls), Canggu (beaches and cafés) and Uluwatu (clifftop sunsets and the Kecak fire dance), but it's tight for adding island day trips — save Nusa Penida for a 7-day-plus trip.
**What's the best way to split 5 days in Bali?** Base in two areas to avoid daily long drives: two nights in Ubud for culture and nature, then two or three nights in Canggu or near Uluwatu for the coast. The Ubud-to-south transfer takes about 1.5–2 hours depending on traffic.
**How do I get around Bali for 5 days?** Use the Grab and Gojek apps for affordable cars and scooter-taxis, or hire a private driver for full-day sightseeing (around IDR 700,000–900,000/day). Renting a scooter is cheap and flexible but only advisable if you're a confident rider — traffic is the main risk.
**Do I need a visa and pay a tourist tax?** Yes to both for most nationalities: the e-VOA is IDR 500,000 (~US$35) for 30 days, and Bali's tourist levy is a one-time IDR 150,000 (~US$10). Apply for the e-VOA at evisa.imigrasi.go.id and pay the levy via the official Love Bali portal before arrival.
**How much does 5 days in Bali cost?** Expect roughly US$200–300 total per person on a budget (guesthouses, local food, Grab) or US$500–700 mid-range (boutique stays, a private driver, nicer restaurants), excluding flights. The Kecak dance ticket and temple entries add only a few dollars each.

## 3. 7-days-bali-solo-travellers

★ **Is Bali safe for solo travellers?** Yes — Bali is one of Asia's most popular and welcoming destinations for solo travellers, including solo women. The biggest real risk is scooter accidents, so ride cautiously or use Grab/Gojek. Standard precautions apply: watch your drink on nights out and use registered drivers late at night.
**Where should solo travellers stay in Bali?** Canggu and Ubud are the best bases for solo travel, with sociable hostels, co-working cafés and easy group tours for meeting people. Finish near Uluwatu for a quieter surf-and-sunset few days. Avoid spreading across too many areas — pick two hubs.
**Is Bali good for meeting other travellers?** Very. Canggu's hostels, surf schools and co-working spaces, plus Ubud's yoga studios and cooking classes, make it easy to meet people solo. Group day tours to Nusa Penida or the Mount Batur sunrise trek are reliable ways to connect with other travellers.
**How much does a solo week in Bali cost?** Solo travellers can do a week from about US$300–450 on a budget (hostels, local food, scooters and a couple of tours), or US$700–1,000 mid-range with private rooms and drivers. Solo travel costs a little more per head than sharing, mainly on accommodation.
**Do I need a visa and pay the Bali tourist tax?** Yes — the e-VOA costs IDR 500,000 (~US$35) for 30 days (extendable once), and Bali's one-time tourist levy is IDR 150,000 (~US$10). Both can be arranged online before you fly via evisa.imigrasi.go.id and the Love Bali portal.

## 4. 7-days-bali-couples

**What's the most romantic area to stay in Bali for couples?** Ubud for jungle spas and rice-terrace views, then Uluwatu for clifftop resorts and sunset dinners, are the most romantic bases. A Nusa Penida day trip adds dramatic scenery. Splitting your week between two areas keeps the trip varied without constant repacking.
★ **Is Nusa Penida worth a day trip for couples?** Yes. Nusa Penida's Kelingking Beach viewpoint and snorkelling with manta rays are among Bali's most spectacular experiences. Fast boats from Sanur take about 30–45 minutes. Book a private tour with a driver (around IDR 700,000–1,200,000) so you're not crammed into a group on rough island roads.
**When is the best time for a romantic Bali trip?** April to October (dry season) offers the clearest skies for sunsets and calm seas for island trips. May, June and September are ideal — great weather with fewer crowds than the July–August peak, meaning quieter beaches and easier restaurant bookings.
**How much does a romantic week in Bali cost for two?** Mid-range couples spend roughly US$1,400–2,500 for the week (boutique or villa stays, private drivers, nice dinners, a Nusa Penida trip), excluding flights. Luxury resorts in Ubud and Uluwatu push well above that; budget couples can do it for around US$800–1,200.
**Do we need a visa and is there a tourist tax?** Yes for most nationalities: each traveller needs an e-VOA (IDR 500,000 / ~US$35, 30 days) and pays Bali's one-time tourist levy (IDR 150,000 / ~US$10). Apply for both online before you fly to avoid airport queues on arrival.

## 5. 10-days-bali-gili-islands

★ **How do I get from Bali to the Gili Islands, and how much is the fast boat?** Fast boats run daily from Bali (Padang Bai, Sanur or Serangan) to Gili Trawangan in about 1.5–3 hours, costing roughly US$25–42 (around IDR 400,000–650,000) one way. Padang Bai is the fastest departure; book ahead in peak season and expect rougher afternoon crossings.
**Is 10 days enough for Bali and the Gili Islands?** Yes — 10 days lets you enjoy Bali (Ubud, Canggu, Uluwatu) and still spend 3–4 nights on the Gilis without feeling rushed. Allow most of a day for each fast-boat transfer, and don't try to add Lombok or Nusa Penida as well in this time.
**Which Gili island should I choose?** Gili Trawangan is the liveliest, with bars, dive shops and the most accommodation; Gili Air is the relaxed middle ground with good cafés and snorkelling; Gili Meno is the quietest and most romantic. All three are car-free — you get around on foot, bicycle or pony cart.
**When is the best time to visit Bali and the Gilis?** April to October (dry season) is ideal: calm seas for the fast boat and the clearest water for snorkelling turtles around the Gilis. Avoid January–February peak rains, which can disrupt boat crossings and cloud the snorkelling.
**Do I need a visa and pay the Bali tourist tax?** Yes — the e-VOA is IDR 500,000 (~US$35) for 30 days, and Bali's one-time tourist levy is IDR 150,000 (~US$10). The Gili Islands are in Lombok province, so no separate entry fee applies beyond your fast-boat ticket.

## 6. 10-days-bali-lombok-gili-islands

**Is 10 days enough for Bali, Lombok and the Gili Islands?** Yes, if you keep it simple: a few days in Bali, a fast boat to the Gilis, and a short hop to Lombok. Ten days covers the highlights but not Rinjani's multi-day trek — choose either a relaxed island circuit or one big adventure, not both.
★ **How do I travel between Bali, the Gili Islands and Lombok?** Fast boats connect Bali (Padang Bai/Sanur) to the Gilis and Lombok in about 1.5–3 hours for roughly US$25–42 one way; Gili-to-Lombok hops take 15–45 minutes. Within Lombok, use Grab or a private driver — public transport is limited.
**Which Gili island suits this trip?** For a Bali–Lombok circuit, Gili Air is a convenient, relaxed choice with good snorkelling and easy onward boats to Lombok. Choose Gili Trawangan if you want nightlife, or Gili Meno for the quietest stay. All three are car-free and walkable.
**When is the best time for this trip?** April to October (dry season) gives the calmest seas for the multiple boat crossings and the best snorkelling and surf. The wet season (November–March) can cancel fast boats at short notice, which is disruptive on an island-hopping route.
**Do I need a visa and pay a tourist tax?** Yes — the e-VOA costs IDR 500,000 (~US$35) for 30 days, plus Bali's one-time IDR 150,000 (~US$10) levy. Lombok and the Gilis charge no separate tourist tax, so your main extra costs are the fast-boat tickets.

## 7. 7-days-lombok-gili-islands

**Is 7 days enough for Lombok and the Gili Islands?** Yes. A week covers Kuta Lombok's surf beaches, a Senaru waterfall-and-Rinjani-foothills day, and 2–3 nights on the Gilis. It's not enough for the full 2–3 day Rinjani summit trek, so plan that as a dedicated add-on if it's a priority.
**What are the highlights of Lombok beyond the Gilis?** Kuta Lombok's white-sand surf beaches (Tanjung Aan, Selong Belanak), the Sendang Gile and Tiu Kelep waterfalls near Senaru, and the foothills of Mount Rinjani are the main draws. Lombok is quieter and more rural than Bali, with excellent, uncrowded beaches.
★ **How much is the fast boat from Lombok to the Gili Islands?** Public boats from Bangsal harbour to the Gilis take 15–45 minutes and cost only a few dollars (around IDR 20,000–40,000 for the local ferry); private fast boats and resort transfers cost more. From Bali directly, fast boats to the Gilis run about US$25–42 one way.
**Which Gili island is best for this trip?** Gili Trawangan for nightlife and dive shops, Gili Air for a relaxed balance of cafés and snorkelling, Gili Meno for romance and quiet. From Lombok's Bangsal harbour all three are a short hop, so you can easily day-trip between them.
**When is the best time to visit Lombok and the Gilis?** April to October (dry season) is best for surf, trekking and snorkelling, with the calmest boat crossings. Rinjani's trekking routes typically close during the rainy season (roughly January–March) for safety, so time a trek for the dry months.

## 8. 7-days-yogyakarta-east-java

★ **Can I visit Mount Bromo and Ijen on the same trip?** Yes — Bromo and the Ijen blue-flame crater are both in East Java and are commonly combined over 2–3 days, usually Yogyakarta → Bromo (sunrise) → Ijen (pre-dawn hike) → Banyuwangi or Bali. Both involve very early starts, so build in rest time.
★ **What are the Borobudur and Prambanan entrance fees in 2026?** Borobudur costs about IDR 455,000 for foreign visitors (temple grounds; the sunrise/upper-temple climb is a separate, pricier ticket around IDR 1,000,000), and Prambanan is IDR 400,000. The combined Borobudur–Prambanan ticket was discontinued in 2025, so you now buy each separately.
★ **What are the Bromo and Ijen entrance fees?** Mount Bromo's foreign-visitor fee is about IDR 220,000 on weekdays and IDR 320,000 on weekends/holidays. Ijen's entrance fee is lower (roughly IDR 100,000–150,000). Most travellers visit on a guided tour that bundles the park fees, jeep and transfers.
**How do I get from Yogyakarta to Mount Bromo?** It's a long haul east — about 8–10 hours by private car, or a train to Probolinggo/Malang followed by a jeep transfer to the Bromo area. Most travellers book a multi-day Bromo–Ijen tour from Yogyakarta that handles all transport and the pre-dawn jeep.
**When is the best time for Yogyakarta and East Java?** The dry season (April–October) gives the clearest Bromo sunrise and safest volcano hikes; the Ijen blue flames are only visible pre-dawn year-round. Avoid the rainy months, when cloud and mud spoil the volcano views and make trails slippery.

## 9. 15-days-java-bali

**Is 15 days enough for Java and Bali?** Yes — two weeks comfortably links Yogyakarta's temples (Borobudur, Prambanan), the East Java volcanoes (Bromo, Ijen) and Bali (Ubud, Uluwatu), with realistic travel time between regions. It's a classic overland-then-island route; rushing it into less than 12 days makes the long Java legs exhausting.
★ **What do the main temple and volcano entry fees cost?** Budget for Borobudur (~IDR 455,000 foreign), Prambanan (IDR 400,000), Mount Bromo (IDR 220,000 weekday / 320,000 weekend) and Ijen (~IDR 100,000–150,000). The Borobudur–Prambanan combo ticket ended in 2025, so each is now separate. Guided tours often bundle the volcano fees.
**How do I travel from Java to Bali?** Most travellers go overland east through Java (Yogyakarta → Bromo → Ijen → Banyuwangi), then take the short ferry from Ketapang to Gilimanuk in Bali, or fly Surabaya/Yogyakarta to Denpasar to save time. Trains link Java's cities comfortably and cheaply.
**When is the best time for a Java and Bali trip?** April to October (dry season) is ideal for the volcano sunrises and Bali's beaches. The shoulder months of May, June and September balance good weather with fewer crowds at Borobudur and Bromo, which get busy in July–August.
**How much does 15 days in Java and Bali cost?** Mid-range travellers spend roughly US$1,200–2,000 per person for two weeks (decent hotels, guided volcano tours, domestic transport, temple fees), excluding international flights. Budget travellers using trains, guesthouses and shared tours can do it for around US$700–1,000.

## 10. 14-days-indonesia-bali-java-komodo

**Is 14 days enough for Bali, Java and Komodo?** Yes — two weeks covers Bali, Java's highlights (Yogyakarta, Bromo, Ijen) and a Komodo boat trip, but it relies on domestic flights to connect the regions. It's a fast-paced "greatest hits" route; add days if you want downtime rather than constant moving.
★ **How do I get between Bali, Java and Komodo?** Domestic flights do the heavy lifting: Yogyakarta and Surabaya connect Java to Bali in about 1–1.5 hours, and Bali to Labuan Bajo (the Komodo gateway) is roughly 1h10–1h20. Book these in advance — Labuan Bajo flights sell out and prices rise in peak season.
★ **What's the Komodo National Park entry fee?** In 2026 Komodo uses a single bundled ticket per route — about IDR 650,000 per person for the Komodo Island route or IDR 900,000 for routes including Rinca and Padar, covering park entry, ranger and site fees. Pre-booking via the SiORA app or a licensed operator is required.
**When is the best time for this trip?** April to October (dry season) is best across all three regions — clear Bromo sunrises, calm Komodo seas and dry Bali weather. July–August is peak; May, June and September offer similar conditions with fewer crowds and easier Labuan Bajo flight availability.
**How much does 14 days across Bali, Java and Komodo cost?** Mid-range travellers spend roughly US$1,800–3,000 per person, including domestic flights, a 2–3 day Komodo boat trip, guided volcano tours and the park fees. Budget travellers sharing boat and tour costs can manage around US$1,100–1,600, excluding international flights.

## 11. 5-days-labuan-bajo-komodo

★ **What's the Komodo National Park entrance fee in 2026?** Komodo now uses one bundled ticket per route: about IDR 650,000 per person for the Komodo Island route, or IDR 900,000 for routes including Rinca and Padar, covering park entry, ranger fees and major sites. The old IDR 3.75M conservation fee was cancelled and not reinstated.
★ **Do I need to pre-book to enter Komodo National Park?** Yes. From April 2026 all visitors must pre-book through the official SiORA app or a licensed operator, and a daily cap of 1,000 visitors applies. The simplest route is to book your boat trip with a registered operator who arranges the permit for you.
**Is 5 days enough for Labuan Bajo and Komodo?** Yes. Five days covers a 2-day/1-night or 3-day boat trip through the park — Padar Island's viewpoint, Komodo or Rinca dragons, Pink Beach and Manta Point snorkelling — plus arrival and departure days, without feeling rushed.
**When can you see Komodo dragons?** Year-round. Dragons are visible on both Komodo and Rinca islands all year; the dry season (April–October) brings the best boat conditions and calmest seas. They're most active in cooler morning hours, so start ranger-guided treks early.
★ **How do I get to Labuan Bajo?** Fly into Labuan Bajo (Komodo Airport): it's about 1h10–1h20 from Bali (Denpasar) and there are connections via Jakarta. From the airport it's a short drive to the harbour, where Komodo boat trips and liveaboards depart. Book flights early — seats are limited and sell out in peak season.

## 12. 10-days-komodo-flores

★ **Is the Trans-Flores overland route worth it, and how long are the drives?** Yes — the Labuan Bajo → Ruteng → Bajawa → Moni (Kelimutu) → Ende route is one of Indonesia's great road trips, through rice terraces, traditional villages and volcanoes. Expect 3–5 hours of winding mountain driving between most stops, so plan an overnight at each.
**Should I self-drive or hire a driver across Flores?** Hire a local driver-guide. The Trans-Flores road is paved but extremely winding, with landslides possible in the wet season, and signage and roadside help are limited. A driver (arranged in Labuan Bajo) is affordable, safer, and lets you enjoy the scenery and stop freely.
★ **What's the Komodo National Park fee for this trip?** If you add a Komodo boat trip from Labuan Bajo, the 2026 bundled fee is about IDR 650,000 (Komodo route) or IDR 900,000 (Rinca/Padar route) per person, pre-booked via the SiORA app or a licensed operator. The overland Flores legs have no equivalent park fee.
**Is 10 days enough for Komodo and Flores?** Yes — 10 days lets you combine a Komodo boat trip from Labuan Bajo with the overland drive across Flores to Kelimutu's coloured crater lakes near Moni, ending in Ende. It's a full itinerary; trim a stop if you want a slower pace.
**When is the best time to visit Komodo and Flores?** April to October (dry season) is best — calm seas for the Komodo boats and drier, safer roads for the Trans-Flores drive. The wet season brings landslide risk on mountain roads and rougher crossings, so the dry months are strongly preferable.

## 13. 14-days-raja-ampat-divers

★ **How much are the Raja Ampat permits in 2026?** Two official fees apply: the Marine Park Entry Permit costs IDR 700,000 for foreign visitors (valid 12 months), plus a IDR 300,000 Visitor Entry Ticket (single visit) — about IDR 1,000,000 (US$65) total. Both are mandatory, even for short stays, and fund local conservation.
★ **When is the best time to dive Raja Ampat?** October to April offers the best diving, with calm seas, 28–30°C water and peak manta season around December–February. The reefs are diveable most of the year, but July–August can bring stronger winds and rougher crossings to outer sites like Wayag and Misool.
★ **Liveaboard or homestay in Raja Ampat?** Liveaboards reach the best remote sites (Misool, Wayag) and suit serious divers, but cost more (often US$300+/day). Homestays around Kri and Gam are far cheaper, support local communities, and give excellent Dampier Strait diving — ideal for independent divers on a budget.
★ **How do I get to Raja Ampat?** Fly to Sorong (West Papua) via Jakarta, Makassar or Manado, then take the ferry from Sorong to Waisai (about 2 hours, a few daily departures). From Waisai, boats transfer you to your homestay or resort. Build in buffer time — missed ferry connections can cost a day.
**Is 14 days too long for Raja Ampat?** No, for divers it's ideal. Two weeks allows diving across multiple areas (Dampier Strait, Piaynemo, Misool), proper surface intervals between dives, and a weather buffer for the boat crossings. Non-divers may prefer a shorter trip focused on snorkelling and the Piaynemo viewpoints.

## 14. 14-days-bali-komodo-sumba

★ **How do I get from Bali to Sumba and Komodo?** All by short domestic flight: Bali to Sumba (Tambolaka) takes about 1h–1h10, and Bali to Labuan Bajo (Komodo) about 1h10–1h20. There's no practical direct Sumba–Komodo flight, so most itineraries route via Bali between the two islands.
**Why add Sumba to a Bali and Komodo trip?** Sumba offers something Bali and Komodo don't: empty white-sand beaches, dramatic surf, traditional Marapu villages with megalithic tombs, and iconic waterfalls like Lapopu. It's remote and undeveloped, which is the appeal — pair it for culture and seclusion after Komodo's wildlife and Bali's buzz.
★ **What's the Komodo National Park fee?** In 2026 it's a single bundled ticket per route — about IDR 650,000 (Komodo route) or IDR 900,000 (Rinca/Padar route) per person — pre-booked through the SiORA app or a licensed operator, covering park entry, ranger and site fees.
**When is the best time for this trip?** April to October (dry season) suits all three: calm Komodo seas, dry weather on Sumba and sunny Bali. Sumba's surf is best in the dry season too. Avoid the wet months when Komodo crossings get rough and Sumba's dirt roads turn muddy.
**How much does 14 days across Bali, Komodo and Sumba cost?** Mid-range travellers spend roughly US$2,000–3,500 per person, including domestic flights, a Komodo boat trip, Sumba's higher accommodation costs and the park fees. Sumba's limited, often boutique lodging raises the average; budget options there are scarce.

The mix of wildlife, culture and seclusion makes it a standout honeymoon route.

## 16. 15-days-sumatra

★ **Is Bukit Lawang orangutan trekking ethical, and what does it cost?** Bukit Lawang offers genuinely wild orangutan sightings in Gunung Leuser National Park; choose operators who keep distance, don't feed wildlife, and follow park rules. A full-day trek runs about €70 (IDR 1.15M) and multi-day treks around €100/day, usually including the park permit, guide and meals.
★ **How do I get from Medan to Lake Toba?** Lake Toba is about 170 km from Medan — roughly 4–7 hours depending on transport. A private car via the toll road reaches Parapat (the ferry gateway) in about 3–4 hours; local buses take 4–5 hours. From Parapat, a short ferry crosses to Samosir Island.
**Is 15 days enough for Sumatra?** Yes — two weeks comfortably links Bukit Lawang (orangutans), Berastagi (volcanoes), and Lake Toba/Samosir (Batak culture and crater-lake relaxation), with realistic overland travel between them. Sumatra is large and journeys are slow, so 15 days is about right; don't try to add the far south or west.
**When is the best time to visit Sumatra?** The drier months (roughly May to September) are best for jungle trekking and volcano views, with more reliable trail conditions in Gunung Leuser. Sumatra is wetter year-round than Bali, so pack for rain regardless and expect humidity in the lowland jungle.
**Do I need vaccinations or malaria precautions for Sumatra?** Possibly — parts of rural Sumatra (including jungle areas) carry malaria risk, unlike Bali. Consult a travel-health clinic 4–6 weeks before departure about antimalarials and routine/recommended vaccinations. Bring insect repellent and cover up at dusk; this is general guidance, not medical advice.

## 17. 20-days-across-indonesia

**Is 20 days enough to see Bali, Java, Komodo and Lombok?** Yes — three weeks links all four well: Bali, Java's volcanoes and temples, a Komodo boat trip, and Lombok with the Gilis, connected by domestic flights. It's an active "highlights" route across the archipelago; it's not a slow trip, so expect regular travel days.
★ **How many domestic flights will I need?** Around three to four — typically Bali↔Yogyakarta/Surabaya for Java (~1–1.5h), Bali↔Labuan Bajo for Komodo (~1h10–1h20), and a Bali–Lombok hop or fast boat. Booking these early keeps costs down and avoids sold-out Labuan Bajo seats in peak season.
**What's the total budget for 20 days across Indonesia?** Mid-range travellers spend roughly US$2,500–4,000 per person, including domestic flights, a Komodo boat trip, guided volcano tours, park fees and varied accommodation. Budget travellers using guesthouses, trains and shared tours can manage around US$1,500–2,200, excluding international flights.
**When is the best time for this multi-region trip?** April to October (dry season) works best across all four regions — clear volcano sunrises, calm Komodo seas and good island weather. The wet season (November–March) risks fast-boat cancellations and clouded volcano views, which is disruptive on a tightly linked route.
**How do I get around within each region?** Use domestic flights between regions; within them, Grab and Gojek apps cover Bali and Java's cities, private drivers handle volcano and temple days, and fast boats link Lombok and the Gilis. Distances look short but roads are slow, so build in realistic transfer times.

## 18. 20-days-wild-indonesia

★ **What's the Tanjung Puting orangutan experience in Kalimantan?** You explore Tanjung Puting National Park by traditional liveaboard "klotok" riverboat over 2–3 days, visiting orangutan feeding stations and sleeping on deck under the stars. Trips depart from Kumai (near Pangkalan Bun airport); a private klotok with guide, crew and meals is the classic way to do it.
**What is there to see in Tana Toraja, Sulawesi?** Tana Toraja is famous for its dramatic funeral ceremonies, cliff-side graves, hanging effigies (tau-tau) and boat-shaped tongkonan houses. It's a deeply cultural highland region reached overland from Makassar (about 8 hours by road). The elaborate funeral season peaks around July–August.
★ **What are the Raja Ampat permit fees?** Two mandatory fees: the Marine Park Entry Permit (IDR 700,000 for foreigners, valid 12 months) plus a IDR 300,000 Visitor Entry Ticket — about IDR 1,000,000 (US$65) total. Access is via a flight to Sorong then a ~2-hour ferry to Waisai.
**When is the best time for this wild Indonesia route?** The dry season (roughly May–October) is best for Kalimantan's rivers and Sulawesi's roads, while Raja Ampat dives best October–April — so the shoulder months around September–October balance all three. This is a remote, logistics-heavy route; build in buffer days for connections.
**How do I get around on this trip?** Mostly by domestic flights between the regions (via hubs like Pangkalan Bun, Makassar and Sorong), then local transport on the ground — klotok boats in Kalimantan, a private driver in Sulawesi, and ferries/boats in Raja Ampat. Internal flights are essential given the distances.

## 19. 21-days-indonesia-beyond-bali

**Is three weeks enough to see Indonesia beyond Bali?** Three weeks comfortably links Java, Flores, Lombok and a Sumatra highlight without backtracking — but Indonesia is vast, so this is a "greatest hits beyond Bali" route rather than comprehensive. Expect several domestic flights, since overland travel between these regions is impractical.
★ **How do I travel between Java, Flores, Lombok and Sumatra?** Domestic flights are essential: most legs route through hubs like Jakarta, Surabaya, Bali or Labuan Bajo. Within regions you'll use trains and drivers in Java, boats in Flores/Lombok, and overland transport in Sumatra. Book the inter-region flights early to control cost and availability.
**Do I need vaccinations or antimalarials for this trip?** Possibly — rural parts of Flores and Sumatra carry malaria risk, unlike Bali and most of Java. See a travel-health clinic 4–6 weeks ahead about antimalarials and recommended vaccinations, and pack insect repellent. This is general guidance, not medical advice.
**When is the best time for a beyond-Bali trip?** April to October (dry season) works across most of these regions — best for Java's volcanoes, Flores' roads and Lombok's beaches. Sumatra is wetter year-round, so expect some rain there regardless; the mid-year dry months are the safest overall bet.
**How much does 21 days beyond Bali cost?** Mid-range travellers spend roughly US$2,800–4,500 per person over three weeks, driven largely by the multiple domestic flights plus accommodation and tours. Budget travellers using guesthouses, public transport and shared tours can do it for around US$1,800–2,500, excluding international flights.

## 20. 30-days-indonesia-ultimate

**Is one month enough to see Indonesia?** One month lets you cover Indonesia's highlights across six or seven regions — Bali, Java, Komodo/Flores, Lombok/Gili, Sumatra, Sulawesi and Raja Ampat — but with 17,000 islands, even 30 days is a highlights tour built around domestic flights, not exhaustive. It's the most complete single trip most travellers will do.
★ **How many domestic flights will I need for a month in Indonesia?** Expect roughly six to eight, since the regions are far apart and overland links are impractical — e.g. Bali↔Java, Bali↔Labuan Bajo (~1h10–1h20), and longer hops to Sumatra, Sulawesi and Sorong (for Raja Ampat). Booking early is essential for cost and availability.
**What's the total budget for 30 days in Indonesia?** Mid-range travellers spend roughly US$4,000–7,000 per person for the month, including six to eight domestic flights, a Komodo boat trip, Raja Ampat permits and diving, and varied accommodation. Budget travellers can do it for around US$2,800–4,000; luxury easily exceeds US$10,000.
**Which regions should I cut if I have less than a month?** Drop the hardest-to-reach first: Raja Ampat and Sulawesi add the most flights and cost. A 2–3 week version keeping Bali, Java, Komodo/Flores and Lombok still covers the iconic sights — see our [20-day](https://exploreindonesia.ai/trips/20-days-across-indonesia) and [14-day Bali–Java–Komodo](https://exploreindonesia.ai/trips/14-days-indonesia-bali-java-komodo) routes.
**When is the best time for a one-month Indonesia trip?** Aim for the dry season (April–October) for Java's volcanoes, Komodo and the islands. Note Raja Ampat dives best October–April, so a September–October trip best overlaps all regions. Whenever you go, some regional rain is likely somewhere on a route this big.
