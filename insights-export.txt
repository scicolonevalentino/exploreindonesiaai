Ubud > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] At the Tegalalang rice terraces only the first official ticket booth is mandatory; the 'donation' checkpoints further along the path are informal collections by individual landowners.
  - [Local knowledge] Walk the Campuhan Ridge at 6–7am: flat, free and cool. By 9am it's fully exposed with no shade for the whole 2km.
  - [Easy to miss] At Tirta Empul you must wear a provided sarong and enter the purification pools from a specific spring order — joining the queue at the wrong fountain is a common faux pas.

Canggu > insights
  - [Local knowledge] Canggu's shortcut roads gridlock 8–10am and 5–7pm — a 3km hop can take 40 minutes. Use a scooter at peak hours, never a car.
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] Canggu's beaches are grey volcanic sand with strong rip currents — it's a surf-and-cafe town, not the white-sand swimming Bali people picture.

Seminyak > insights
  - [Easy to miss] Kuta and Legian have powerful rip currents — swim only between the red-and-yellow flags where Balawista lifeguards patrol, not wherever looks calm.

Uluwatu > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] The Uluwatu temple monkeys deliberately snatch glasses and phones to 'trade' back for food — keep everything zipped away, not just held tight.
  - [Easy to miss] Uluwatu's beaches (Padang Padang, Bingin) sit at the bottom of steep cliff stairs and nearly vanish at high tide — check the tide chart first.

Mount Batur > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] In rainy season (Dec–Feb) the Batur summit sunrise is often clouded out and a licensed local guide is mandatory by cooperative rule — independent hiking isn't allowed.
  - [Easy to miss] Batur treks start with a 2am pickup and it's genuinely cold at 1,700m — bring a warm layer; the tropics assumption catches almost everyone out.

East Bali > insights
  - [Local knowledge] Amed and Tulamben on the east coast have black volcanic sand and the USAT Liberty shipwreck dive straight off the beach — calm, and far less crowded than the south.

Nusa Penida > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] Kelingking's famous 'T-Rex' shot is from the clifftop; reaching the actual beach is a steep, exposed 30-minute climb down and a brutal one back up.
  - [Local knowledge] Penida's west spots (Kelingking, Angel's Billabong) and east (Atuh, Diamond) are too far apart on rough roads for one relaxed day — pick one side.
  - [Easy to miss] ATMs on Nusa Penida are scarce and often empty — withdraw all the cash you'll need in Sanur before the boat.

Nusa Lembongan > insights
  - [Local knowledge] Lembongan and neighbouring Ceningan are joined by the bright-yellow Yellow Bridge — you can scooter both islands in a day; many travelers don't realize they're that close.

Gili Islands > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] There are no motorized vehicles on the Gilis — transport is bicycle or horse-cart (cidomo). 'Getting a taxi' isn't possible; plan to walk or cycle.
  - [Local knowledge] Gili Trawangan is the party island, Gili Air the balanced one, Gili Meno the quiet honeymoon island — choosing the wrong one for your vibe is the classic mistake.
  - [Easy to miss] The Gilis have no hospital and ATMs that frequently run dry — bring any medication you need and enough cash before you cross.

Yogyakarta > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] Climbing Borobudur now needs a timed ticket (daily cap) plus provided sandals on the upper levels — booking ahead is essential; walk-ups are often turned away.
  - [Local knowledge] The classic Borobudur 'sunrise' is usually viewed from neighbouring Setumbu Hill, not the monument itself, which opens later than sunrise tours imply.

Mount Bromo > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] The iconic Bromo photo is shot from Penanjakan viewpoint looking AT Bromo — the crater rim is a separate jeep stop plus a hike across the ash sea.
  - [Easy to miss] Bromo's viewpoint sits near 2,700m and pre-dawn temperatures drop close to freezing — the single biggest thing tropics-bound travelers underpack for.

Ijen > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] Ijen's blue flames are only visible in darkness, needing a ~1am start, and a proper gas mask is essential — the sulfuric crater fumes are genuinely hazardous.

Komodo > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] The deer, buffalo and wild boar near the ranger stations are the dragons' prey — that's why rangers keep you in tight groups even when the dragons look sleepy.
  - [Local knowledge] Padar Island's famous three-bay viewpoint is a sweaty 30-minute stair climb — do it at sunrise, before the heat and the day-boat crowds.
  - [Easy to miss] Boats leave Labuan Bajo before the harbour warungs open — arrange a takeaway breakfast the night before or you'll go hungry until midday.

Kelimutu > insights
  - [Local knowledge] Kelimutu's three crater lakes change colour unpredictably — turquoise, green, black, sometimes red. There's no 'best' season; you get whatever colours that week brings.

Lombok > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] Rinjani is a serious 2–3 day trek to 3,726m, not a day hike, and it closes entirely in rainy season (roughly Jan–Mar) — always check the official status first.
  - [Local knowledge] Kuta Lombok (not Kuta Bali) is a quiet surf town with white-sand bays like Tanjung Aan and Mawun — a completely different vibe from its Bali namesake.

Bukit Lawang > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] Bukit Lawang's orangutans are semi-wild; ethical guides won't feed them or guarantee sightings — operators promising 'guaranteed close-up orangutans' are the ones to avoid.

Lake Toba > insights
  - [Local knowledge] Lake Toba is a vast volcanic caldera; most travelers base on Samosir Island (Tuk Tuk) in the middle, reached by the standard ferry from Parapat — not the mainland shore.

Mentawai > insights
  - [Local knowledge] The Mentawai Islands are a world-class but advanced surf destination reached by ferry from Padang — powerful reef breaks, not beginner-friendly beach waves.

Raja Ampat > insights
  - [AI assistants get this wrong] Raja Ampat has almost no ATMs or Western-style resorts — most stays are basic cash-only homestays, and you must buy the marine-park entry permit. Plan cash and logistics carefully.
  - [Local knowledge] The signature Wayag karst photo means a steep, hand-over-hand 30-minute scramble up sharp limestone — wear grippy shoes; gloves genuinely help.

Sulawesi > insights
  - [Local knowledge] Toraja's elaborate funeral ceremonies are often open to respectful visitors — bring a traditional gift (sugar or cigarettes) and go with a local guide; it's a solemn event, not a show.
  - [Easy to miss] Bunaken's reef wall drops off just metres from shore — superb snorkeling straight off the boat, no deep dive needed to see the wall.

Banda Islands > insights
  - [Local knowledge] The Banda Islands were once the world's only source of nutmeg — the historic forts and plantations are walkable, but flights via Ambon are limited, so build in buffer days.
